# IELTS Study Suite

100 free study apps for **IELTS Academic** and **IELTS General Training**: Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking, vocabulary and planning. Every app is a Windows program that needs no install and works offline, and every app also runs in the browser at **https://ielts-study-suite.vercel.app**. No sign-up, no ads; your progress stays on your device.

- **Listening (20 apps):** generated Part 1 phone calls with forms to fill, spelled names and postcodes, numbers and dates, generated maps to label, distractor training, 24 original recordings for Parts 2, 3 and 4 read by your computer's voices, and a timed Listening test with an approximate band.
- **Reading (20 apps):** six academic passages with every question type (matching headings, matching information, sentence endings, summary completion, short answers, multiple choice, True/False/Not Given), General Training everyday and workplace texts, skimming and scanning, and a timed Reading test for either module.
- **Writing (20 apps):** generated line graphs, bar charts, pie charts and tables for Task 1, processes and maps, General Training letters, 48 Task 2 prompts in five essay types, planning, introductions, paragraphs, cohesion and common errors.
- **Speaking (20 apps):** Part 1 topics, 40 Part 2 cue cards with a one-minute preparation timer, Part 3 discussion, a full Speaking test read aloud and recorded on your device, fluency, pronunciation and stress practice.
- **Vocabulary and planning (20 apps):** academic and topic words with spaced repetition, collocations, grammar, British and American English, a band score calculator, a mock test of all four skills, a study planner, requirements tracker, error log and dashboard.

## How it is built

Each app is plain HTML and JavaScript on a shared kit (3,028 lines), with the app's own code averaging 23 lines. The Windows program is a small Python launcher packed with PyInstaller: it serves the app on 127.0.0.1 only, needs a per-run secret token to save, and opens it in its own Microsoft Edge window. Listening uses the voices built into Windows or the browser; speaking recordings stay on the device.

All content was written for the suite: 24 listening scripts, 6 academic passages, 11 General Training texts, 80 True/False/Not Given and Yes/No/Not Given statements, 48 essay prompts, 24 letter tasks, 30 Part 1 topics, 40 cue cards with 120 Part 3 questions, about 370 academic words, 90 topic words and more. Part 1 calls, maps, number drills and Task 1 charts are generated, so they never run out. None of it is official IELTS material.

## How it was checked

- 2,901 automated tests across the 100 repositories (Node and Python), plus kit tests: the overall band rounding rule checked against a second formula for thousands of combinations, every number from 0 to 9,999 spoken and read back, every generated Part 1 call checked so its answers are said and fit the word limit, and chart facts checked against the numbers.
- A content checker marks 1,162 bank items with the same code the apps use, and confirms that every typed answer actually appears in its recording or passage and keeps to its word limit.
- Every app opened in headless Microsoft Edge, with its self-test pressing through each section.
- Every .exe started and passed its own self-test (pages, token, saving).
- Pages were also reviewed by eye. Between them, the review and the checks found problems that were then fixed: letter answers shown twice (A A) and a form explanation quoting the wrong speaker (seen on screen), a lecture answer that was not said word for word, a short answer over its word limit, and phrases repeated across two banks (caught by the checks).

## Limits

- Not used by real students yet, and the content has not been reviewed by a second teacher or an IELTS examiner.
- Writing and speaking feedback counts what a program can count honestly (length, paragraphs, linking words); it does not give a band. The self-check apps help you judge your own work against the four criteria.
- Computer voices are clear but flatter than real test recordings, and accents depend on the voices installed.
- Raw-score to band conversions use the approximate guide tables IELTS publishes; real tests vary slightly. Some practice tests are shorter than 40 questions and are scaled.
- The format follows IELTS as delivered on computer in 2026. Check ielts.org before your test.
